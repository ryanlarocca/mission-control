### 1.2 What's weakest

1. **It is a conjunction sold as a base case.** Top is in (60%) → recession (50–55%) → credit breaks mid-2027 → Fed cuts late 2027 → SCC trough Q4 2027–Q2 2028. Each link is a coin flip and the housing call sits at the end of the chain. Multiplied through, the full bear path is nearer 25–35% than the ~60% the narrative implies. There is no "base" scenario at all; it's bear (60) vs bull (35–40).
2. **The SCC peak-to-trough is measured on a seasonal series.** SCC monthly medians fall 3–6% every year from May to December. "Peak $2.1M May → $1.92M Sep" is −8.6%, but roughly half of that is normal seasonality. The clean signal is −4% YoY. **A Jan 1 review looking at December medians will see scary prints that are mostly seasonal.** Every price claim must use YoY, a trailing-3-month median vs the same 3 months a year earlier, or Zillow ZHVI for the county (smoothed, seasonally adjusted).
3. **Wrong analog for South Bay housing.** 1973–74 says nothing about SCC houses. The two local precedents are **2000–02** (tech jobs −20%, SCC median roughly −10% to −15% over two years) and **2022–23** (rate shock only: SCC median ~$1.95M May 2022 → ~$1.6M Jan 2023, about −17% in eight months, then new highs by 2024). The thesis trough size is consistent with both, but the *timing* is not: a 2022-style path puts the trough around Q1 2027, which is nearly now; a 2000-style path puts it in 2028. Which analog is running is the single most important fork and should be a scored claim.
4. **"No forced selling" is true for 3%-mortgage owner-occupants and false for the marginal seller in SCC.** Forced or near-forced channels the 2008 comparison misses: bridge-loan flippers at 9–10% carry (that's us); RSU-dependent buyers who stretched in 2024–25; H-1B holders who lose a job and must sell within the 60-day grace period; trust/estate sales from an aging owner base. These are small in count but they set the marginal price in a thin market.
5. **The AI liquidity counter-current is under-weighted.** Anthropic filed its S-1 publicly Oct 26 (ticker ANT, mid/late-Nov listing talk, insiders can trade from day one with staged releases at days 27/48/62/76). SpaceX's final lockup lands Dec 8–9. OpenAI's confidential S-1 is in, CFO points to 2027. The thesis treats SF +17% as the most fragile number, but the IPO calendar can *extend* it into H1 2027 and bid the Peninsula/South Bay $3M+ tier even while the county median falls. The county median can go down while the top tier goes up. Also: the 2000 analog requires the IPO window to slam shut. **If ANT prices and trades above issue, the 2000 analog is not yet running; if it is pulled or breaks issue, that is the strongest single bear confirmation available.**
6. **Local labor is not softening yet.** SCC unemployment 4.3% in Aug 2026 vs 4.7% a year earlier. SCC WARN workers −27% YoY in Q3. San Jose rents +8–10% YoY (Zumper, Apartment List). Rising rents with falling prices means the price-to-rent ratio is compressing, which is a floor-building signal, not a crash signal. The thesis cites national payrolls (+29k) and never looks at the county.
7. **Yield curve is steep, not inverted.** 10-yr ~5.0% vs funds ~3.9%. 2000 and 2007 recessions were preceded by inversions. Hiking into a steep curve with GDP at 3.4% looks more like 1994 or 2022 than 2000. This belongs in the opposite case every month.
8. **Mortgage rate path ignores the spread.** 30-yr minus 10-yr is ~230 bp, well above the ~170 bp norm. Spread compression alone is worth 30–60 bp on mortgages with no move in Treasuries. The thesis's "7%+ through mid-2027" is really a claim about the 10-yr *and* the spread; split them.
9. **"Inventory depleted by 2028" has no mechanism.** If prices fall and rates stay high, inventory does not deplete. Lock-in deepens only if rates fall. The claim is really "rates fall → listings dry up → next leg up," which is the bull mechanism, and it should be dated and scored as such.
10. **SF +17% uses the wrong instrument.** It's a city median. Case-Shiller "San Francisco" is flat YoY through May 2026 (condos about −3%). Note too: **Case-Shiller SF covers five counties and excludes Santa Clara.** It is not an SCC dial. The SCC equivalents are Zillow ZHVI (monthly) and FHFA HPI for the San Jose MSA (quarterly).
11. **Structural demand headwind the thesis never names: immigration.** SCC's population is flat only because international inflow (~+18.7k/yr) offsets domestic outflow (~−13k to −20k/yr). The $100k H-1B fee (Sep 2025) has already cut petitions at Amazon, Google and Meta; DOF's 2026 estimate headline is "federal policy constricting legal international migration." If inflow halves, county population shrinks. Slow, but it is the one headwind that doesn't reverse with a rate cut.
12. **SCC's bear channel is the capex cut itself, not the Fed.** The county's employment is concentrated in exactly the names that pay for or sell the AI build-out (Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Applied, Lam, KLA, Google, Apple, Meta next door). A 2027 capex cut hits SCC employers directly. That makes the "local employer equity basket" and SCC WARN filings more informative than the S&P. Right now that basket is split: Alphabet about −30% from high, Meta about −21%, Nvidia −11 to −13%, Apple at a record. Mixed, not broken.
13. **Timing is stated with false precision.** "Bottom Q3–Q4 2027" for stocks and "Q4 2027–Q2 2028" for SCC are point calls on the end of a five-link chain. v1.1 states them as ranges with probabilities and lets the monthly review move them.

- Tier split for the county: entry (<$1.3M, Halleck's band), core ($1.3–2.0M, Quito's band), $2–3M, $3M+. Track median, DOM and cut share per tier. The thesis's single county median hides the tier divergence the IPO money will create.
- Supply-side detail the thesis never uses: new listings per week, withdrawn/expired share (sellers pulling instead of cutting), relist share, months of supply (SCC is ~1.5 months now; a 2008-style regime is 6+). Also condo vs SFR (condos lead down).
- Mortgage spread (30-yr fixed minus 10-yr) as its own dial.
- Recession early-warning the thesis skips: initial claims 4-week average, Sahm rule, ISM, yield-curve slope.
- Insurance and taxes: FAIR Plan +29.1% effective Oct 15, 2026 (hills, not Blossom Valley); Prop 19 lock-in; reassessment at sale. Low weight, mostly affects the hillside tiers.
- Housing policy: SB 79 transit upzoning effective July 2026 changes land value near Caltrain/VTA stations. Structural, quarterly check only.

### 2.3 Narrative (short, for humans — the claims are the thesis)

Late cycle, yes. ## Part 3 — Tracker game plan (abstract; nothing built)

### 3.1 What it stores

Four kinds of records. Claims and scenario definitions are a versioned config file in the repo (git history is the version log; quarterly rewrite = new version). Readings, checkpoints and weight moves live in the LRG Supabase like every other MC artifact, so the phone page and the review job share one source of truth.

1. **Thesis versions** — the scenario definitions and the claims table above, with version, date, author (chat Claude / me / Ryan). Claims carry: text, initial probability, current probability, resolve-by date, falsifier, category, scenario tag, resolution (true/false/void), resolved date, Brier contribution.
2. **Dial registry + readings** — one row per dial (name, cadence, source, how it's fetched, units, what Bear/Base/Bull each predict for it at the next checkpoint, its noise band). One row per reading (dial, as-of date, value, source URL, raw snippet). Readings are append-only.
3. **Checkpoints** — one row per review (date, type weekly/monthly/quarterly/tripwire, weights before/after, dials that moved, argument for, opposite case, claims resolved, next checkpoints, the Telegram message id).
4. **Weight-move log** — the artifact Ryan called most valuable. Every change to the three weights: date, from → to, the dials cited (with the readings), the argument, the opposite case, and "what would reverse this." Move #0 above is the first entry. A weight never moves without a log row, and a log row must cite at least two readings.

Plus a **data calendar**: upcoming scheduled releases (Nov 6 jobs, Nov 10 CPI, Nov 24 Case-Shiller, Nov 25 PCE, Dec 8–9 SpaceX lockup, Dec 16 FOMC, ANT pricing TBD, late Jan–Feb capex, Jan 28 FOMC...) each tagged with the dial it feeds. The "next checkpoints" line on the card is generated from it.

### 3.2 What it watches (dial registry, by cadence)

**Weekly, local (Monday morning, deterministic script, no LLM).** Source for all MLS dials is the MLSListings public search we already fetch with plain HTTP (`scripts/listing-status.py` / `comps.py` machinery): county SFR active count, pending + active-under-contract count (→ pending ratio), new listings in the last 7 days, median days on site of actives, **price-cut share** (we store every active listing's price each week and detect cuts ourselves, which is more reliable than any vendor's number), withdrawn/relist share, list-to-sale ratio on the week's recently-sold cards. All of it cut by tier (<$1.3M / $1.3–2M / $2–3M / $3M+) and by zip for 95123 and 95130 plus two control zips. Realtor.com's county series on FRED (active count, median DOM, pending ratio, price-reduced count, new listings) serve as the monthly cross-check so a scrape drift is caught. NODs: a free county count weekly until the baseline exists, then the recorder index. Own listings: Halleck and Quito showings, offers, feedback, with the week's DOM and the cut checkpoints from memory. Mechanism is a question for Ryan (see 3.7).

**Monthly, macro (FRED API for nearly all of it).** CPI and core, PCE, payrolls and unemployment, initial claims 4-week average and the Sahm gap, Fed funds target, 10-yr, 30-yr Freddie, the mortgage spread, 2s10s slope, Brent, HY CCC OAS (bond proxy for the loan spread), Case-Shiller national and SF-metro (labelled "not SCC"), Zillow ZHVI for Santa Clara County, SCC unemployment (LAUS), SCC WARN workers (EDD workbook), Bay Area software job postings (Indeed Hiring Lab), San Jose rents (Apartment List CSV). Fitch's private-credit default rate and the Moody's PIK-adjusted figure are quarterly web fetches. The local employer basket is a daily free quote pull, summarised monthly as drawdown from 52-week high.

**Quarterly, structural (LLM-assisted research, written up at the rewrite).** Big-4 2027 capex guidance (transcripts), CAPE and forward P/E, S&P EPS revisions, FHFA HPI for the San Jose MSA, DOF population/migration estimates and USPS net flows, H-1B petition counts, the IPO/lockup calendar refresh, FAIR Plan / insurance, SB 79 or other policy that changes land value.

**Event dials (binary, resolve claims directly).** FOMC decisions, ANT pricing and day-1 close and each staged release, SpaceX Dec lockup, Hormuz status, any named credit event, midterm result (context only).

### 3.4 How it decides drift is meaningful vs noise

Three tests, all must pass before a dial is allowed to "vote" at a checkpoint:

1. **Outside its band.** Level dials with history (inventory, DOM, cut share, rents, NODs, unemployment, WARN) are compared to their own seasonal norm: same week/month over the prior 4–5 years, band = norm ± 1 standard deviation. Market dials (10-yr, mortgage, Brent, CCC OAS, basket) use scenario corridors written into the registry (e.g. Bull corridor Brent <$90 and 10-yr <4.3; Bear corridor CCC OAS >1,500 and basket ≤ −20%) and are read as 10-day averages, never a single print.
2. **Persistent.** Two consecutive readings outside the band, or one reading beyond 2σ. A single week never moves anything.
3. **Directional and scenario-consistent.** Each dial's registry row says which scenario an out-of-band move favors. A dial that is out of band in a direction no scenario predicts is logged as "unexplained" and becomes an opposite-case item, not a vote.

**From votes to weights.** At a monthly checkpoint the review tallies votes by category (local housing, local labor, rates, credit, equities/AI, oil/inflation, events). Weights may move only when ≥3 dials from ≥2 categories vote the same way, or a claim resolves. Cap: 15 points per monthly checkpoint, except when a claim resolves or a tripwire fires (cap 25). The review proposes the move; a hard rule rejects a move whose log row cites fewer than two readings or has an empty opposite-case field. The opposite case must be written against the *new* weights using the *same* month's data, and it must name the strongest reading that disagrees with the move.

**Tripwires** (out-of-cycle checkpoint, pure threshold, no judgment): ANT pulled, postponed, or closes below issue; CCC OAS or loan spread crosses 3,000 bp; a Hormuz reopening or a deal signed; an inter-meeting Fed action; SCC weekly active SFR count crosses ±30% YoY; SCC price-cut share crosses 40%; SCC WARN workers in a month exceed 2× the 2025 monthly average; any claim's falsifier fires early; either of our own listings gets an offer or a cut (that one posts to Ryan, it doesn't move weights).

**Own listings.** Two houses are not a dial. They are logged as ground-truth anecdotes with low weight: the card shows DOM vs the benchmark and the showing count, and the review may cite them in the opposite case ("Quito had 0 showings in a week with county DOM at 26 — is the busy-road discount wider than we think, or is $1.6M the soft tier?"), never as a vote.

**Calibration.** Brier per claim at resolution; at each quarterly rewrite a short table: mean Brier by category, the five biggest surprises, and whether weights were leaning the right way before the data confirmed (did we move Bear up before the WARN print, or after?). That last one is the measure of whether the tracker is earning its keep: early and right, or late and right.
